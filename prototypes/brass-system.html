Brass, the three greys, and a reserve set

Brass is the lightest of the golds, which has a consequence worth seeing before anything else: it cannot carry small text on a light ground. Everything below works around that rather than pretending otherwise.

Recommendation

Neutral grey, not cool grey. Brass is a yellow. Put it on a blue-leaning grey and you rebuild the yellow-against-blue relationship you have now rejected twice, once in the Indigo Night comp and once in the glass hero. It is quieter than those were, but it is the same relationship. Neutral grey removes the blue cast entirely, so the tension cannot arise, and it stays clearly distinct from the cream you ruled out.

Two tones of brass, not one. Brass at #C9A227 fails AA badly as text. It stays as the mark tone for fills, rules, dots and large display figures. A deepened tone carries any small text that has to be brass. This is normal practice and it is the only way brass works at all.

Reserve set of four, used under a written rule. Not part of the palette. Available when a chart genuinely needs more than four series and value-plus-line-style has already failed.

Brass, two tones

Ratios are computed against each candidate ground. AA needs 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text and interface components.

Rule: mark tone is never set below 18px. Text tone is never used as a fill.

The same page on three greys

Identical content and identical brass in all three. Only the grey changes.

Reserve colours

Chosen as siblings of brass rather than as a second palette: same muted saturation, same value band, so that when one appears it reads as another pigment from the same box rather than as a bolted-on brand colour.

  1. Never in the interface. No reserve colour appears in navigation, type, rules, buttons or panels. They exist inside figures only.
  2. Only after value and line style have failed. Four series are separable with ink, brass, mid grey and a dash. Reach for a reserve colour at five or more, not before.
  3. Prussian never sits adjacent to brass in the same figure. That is the yellow-against-blue pairing again. If a chart needs both, put grey between them in the series order.
  4. Introduced in the caption, not in a legend key floating alone. A reserve colour that appears without being named in the caption is decoration.
  5. One reserve colour per figure where possible. Two is a signal the chart should be split.
