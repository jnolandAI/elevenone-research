Thirteen variations on the two that workedthe screen, the point, and what happens when they meet

Both directions are the same object underneath: a lattice of dots whose area is driven by a field. Changing the field, the boundary and whether a point is marked in it produces everything below. Every variant uses the engine's staggered lattice at 15 degrees, and none of them uses opacity to carry a value.

The screen

A field with no marked point. The variable is the boundary and the direction the density runs.

The point

One solid dot with a field around it. The variable is where the point sits in its own distribution, which is a claim about what an estimate is.

Both

The screen's boundary carrying the point's content. These are the only variants that say two things at once: the work is rendered, and it lands somewhere.

How to read the small sizes. The 20 and 16 pixel versions are drawn with a coarser lattice, not scaled down from the large one. Any dot mark needs that, the way a real halftone changes line screen for newsprint. What matters is whether the coarse cut still looks like the same mark, which is where several of these separate.

Ink is #131312 throughout. Nothing here uses a second value that is not a dot.
