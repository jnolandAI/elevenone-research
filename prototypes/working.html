Every firm buries the apparatusWe put it on the page

Sources, assumptions and confidence normally live in an endnote, set small, at the back, where nobody goes. That placement is a decision about what the reader is trusted with. We make the opposite one. A claim travels with what it rests on, what it assumes, and what would have to be true for it to be wrong, and none of that is a footnote or a hover.

Four rows, in the same order, every time. The fourth row is the one no research firm publishes, which is exactly why it is the one worth publishing.

The block sits at three elevations and three tones. That is the signature: a page's own depth tells you how much weight each claim can carry, before you have read a word of it. The standing is also written out, so nothing important lives in shadow alone.

In the flow

Only load-bearing claims get a block. A brief usually has three to six. Everything else is prose, and the reading is not interrupted by an apparatus that had nothing to hold up.

Gross margin is the number a diligence team reaches for first and benchmarks worst. The habit is to pull a sector comparable set, take a median, and treat the distance from it as a finding. That works when the comparable set is genuinely comparable. It stops working the moment size is doing something to the number on its own.

Above $10bn of revenue the median reported gross margin is 29.2%, against 40.8% below $30m. The path between the two is not monotonic.

The middle of the range is the part worth sitting with. The cohorts between $150m and $600m post a higher median than the cohort below them, which is not what a clean size story would predict. Whatever is happening is not a smooth decline, and a brief that drew a line through these six points would be asserting a shape the data does not have.

What does hold cleanly is the spread. Dispersion falls without a reversal across all six cohorts, which is the more useful finding for anyone setting a benchmark: at the small end the median is close to meaningless, because the distribution around it is 72 points wide.

The load path

One object per brief. The conclusion sits on top and the claims that carry it sit underneath, drawn at the weight of their own standing. The thinnest line in the object is always the one holding the least, and a conclusion is never rendered firmer than the weakest claim it rests on.

Conclusion

A single gross margin benchmark misprices companies at both ends of the size range.

Rendered provisional, capped by claim C. The descriptive half of this conclusion is carried by A and B. The prescriptive half needs C, and C is not yet a result.

Claim A

The largest filers report the lowest median gross margin.

Firm · SEC XBRL, n=2,186

Claim B

Dispersion narrows as revenue rises, with no reversal across six cohorts.

Supported · top cohort n=151

Claim C

The gradient is an effect of scale rather than of sector mix.

Provisional · not yet tested

This is the uncomfortable part and it is the point. The brief above cannot conclude what it wants to conclude. There are two honest ways out, and neither of them is restyling the object: test C by re-running the gradient inside sectors, or drop C from the path and publish a conclusion that only describes. Nothing else raises the ceiling.

Rules

  1. Only load-bearing claims get a block. If removing the sentence would not change the conclusion, it is prose. Three to six blocks in a brief. A brief where everything is a claim has no argument, only assertions.
  2. Standing attaches to the claim, not to the number. An exact figure from a complete universe can still carry a provisional reading. The arithmetic and the interpretation get separate blocks whenever they have separate standings.
  3. A conclusion is never rendered firmer than the weakest claim it rests on. The cap is mechanical and it is not overridden. Raising a conclusion means doing the work, not restyling the object.
  4. Breaks if is never left blank and never softened. If we did not test it, the row says we did not test it. A falsifier we cannot name means we do not understand our own claim well enough to publish it.
  5. Nothing important lives in shadow alone. Elevation and tone are the glance channel. The written standing is the real one, and it is always present.
  6. The apparatus is never a hover, a tooltip, or a modal. Anything a reader has to go looking for has been hidden, whatever the interaction cost. It is on the page or it is not published.
  7. Greyscale absolutely. Standing is carried by value, weight and depth. There is no green, amber or red confidence chip here, in any circumstance.

Everything on this page is drawn from one real dataset: the SEC XBRL frames API, us-gaap Revenues and GrossProfit, CY2024 annual, retrieved 2026-08-05. 2,186 registrants report both figures and 221 were excluded for a missing or non-positive denominator. The claims are written as a demonstration of the device rather than as a published brief, and the cohort figures are as reported.
